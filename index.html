The Golden State Turned White

The Rise and Vanishing of California’s Frozen Reservoir

Team: Aurora Cong, Pali Jain, Kitty Ming, Trang Kieu

Same mountains, eight years apart

On April 1, 2015, California's governor stood in a dry meadow in the Sierra Nevada that should have been buried in snow. Eight years later, the same mountains held one of the deepest snowpacks on record. How can one place swing that far?

Every winter, one line

Each line traces one winter: how much of the Sierra Nevada was covered in snow from November to July. Snow arrives, peaks, and melts every year, but how high it climbs and how long it lasts changes a lot. Click years below to compare them, and hover over the chart to read exact values.

Water year = October through September (the 2023 water year runs Oct 2022 – Sep 2023). Snow % is computed from MODIS daily snow cover images, combined over 4 days to see past clouds.

Prototype writeup

What have we done so far?

We chose the NASA MODIS dataset and focused our project on the Sierra Nevada snowpack, using the contrast between the 2015 drought and the 2023 record snow year as our hook. We wrote a Python pipeline that downloads daily MODIS snow cover images from NASA's GIBS API, combines four days at a time to see past clouds, and converts pixel colors back into snow or no-snow using NASA's published colormap. From those images we computed the percent of the Sierra Nevada covered by snow twice a month for every winter from 2013 to 2024. We built the page structure for our explorable explanation and our first interactive D3 visualization, a line chart where readers can pick winters to compare and hover to read exact values.

What will be the most challenging part to design, and why?

The hardest part will be the winter playback map, where readers scrub through a winter and watch snow build up and melt across the Sierra. Each frame is a satellite image, so we need to keep the page fast while loading many images, and we need to decide whether to draw them on a canvas or pre-process them into a lighter format. We also need to explain clearly that our snow percentages come from image pixels rather than raw measurements, without overwhelming readers with technical detail. Finally, connecting the snow story to reservoirs and vegetation downstream without losing the main thread will take careful storytelling and layout.

Data: NASA MODIS (Terra) via Worldview / GIBS. Built with D3.js for DSC 106 at UC San Diego.
